# Carnatify — Complete Project History (June 24 – August 14, 2026)

*Compiled August 19, 2026, from the full contents of the `carnatify` repository: 68 markdown handoff/brief/audit documents, the 275KB `AGENT_LOG.md` coordination log, 172 git commits, the code-structure graph report, and the project's own README/PRD/architecture docs. No email archive or chat export exists in the repo — the closest equivalents are `AGENT_LOG.md` (the agents' shared scratchpad) and the dated `HANDOFF_*.md` files, both of which are used exhaustively below.*

---

## 0. What this document is

Carnatify is a two-month-old (as of this writing) project to build an app that identifies what is being performed at a live Carnatic music concert — the composition, the raga (melodic framework), the lyrics, and their meaning — from audio alone. It started as a personal tool for its creator, Deepti (a practicing Carnatic musician and high-school student with a CS/ML background), and has been evaluated throughout as a possible company.

The project has an unusual structure worth understanding before anything else: almost all of the engineering work was done by a relay of AI agent personas — nicknamed **Fable**, **Codex**, **Opus** (and its "verifier" and "workhorse" sub-seats), and a generic **Claude** seat — coordinating with each other *only* through files committed to the repository (dated `HANDOFF_*.md` documents, numbered `WORKHORSE_*_BRIEF.md` task specs, `CODEX_*.md` reviews, and a single running `AGENT_LOG.md`), because the agents cannot see each other's private conversations. Deepti is the human constant across all of it: the sole authority who can push to git, deploy, spend GPU/API money, swap the production model, or make a musical judgment call. A second named person, **Jon**, appears late in the project as the one concrete real-world user — a Western listener with no prior Carnatic exposure — whose actual, observed confusion at a live concert becomes the project's clearest evidence of demand.

The project's dominant cultural trait, hardened by repeated expensive mistakes, is **radical distrust of its own numbers**. Nearly every major "result" in this repo was later re-audited, and a majority of the headline claims from the project's first month were eventually found to be inflated or outright wrong — not through malice, but through subtle contamination in how training and evaluation data were split. The project's own house rule, stated in almost every handoff, is: *"benchmarks lie, wild clips don't."* This document tries to preserve that arc faithfully — including the retractions — rather than presenting only the flattering version.

## 1. What Carnatify is, in plain terms

From the current `README.md`:

> A Carnatic music identification and enrichment app: given a live recording or an archival clip, it identifies the raga (melodic framework) and the specific composition being performed, and surfaces the composition's lyrics and an English meaning. Built for concert-goers and students of Carnatic music who don't recognize what's being sung in real time.

The core technical bet, present since the original PRD, is that Carnatic music cannot be identified the way Shazam identifies Western pop songs. Shazam-style acoustic fingerprinting matches one specific *recording*; it fails completely on live performance, because Carnatic performance is inherently variable — the same composition, in the same raga, sounds different across artists (different tonic/shruti, different tempo, heavy gamaka ornamentation), and improvised passages (alapana) have no fixed melody to fingerprint at all. Carnatify's answer is to extract the predominant pitch contour, normalize it against the estimated tonic, and match on that normalized melodic representation — the same family of feature CompMusic's own published Carnatic-MIR research uses, and explicitly not Western chroma/harmonic features, which assume chords that monophonic Carnatic music doesn't have.

The product, as it stands today, does two independent things: (1) identify *which* composition is being sung (composition ID), tolerant of different artists/tempos/tonics; and (2) identify the *raga* being performed, which also works during improvised passages that have no fixed composition. Both feed a single display: composition name, composer, raga, tala, original-language lyrics, and an English meaning.

## 2. Origin: the PRD (June 2026)

The project's founding document, `carnatify_prd.md` ("Document version 1.0... Owner: Deepti... Last updated: June 2026"), frames the motivation personally: Deepti had repeatedly sat in a Carnatic concert not knowing which kriti was being sung, not knowing its meaning, with no tool to help. Existing tools all failed for a specific, articulable reason: Shazam-class fingerprinting fails on live/varied performance; static raga-reference apps ("Carnatic Raga" on the App Store) are lookup tools that don't listen to anything; and general Western music-recognition apps use chroma features that don't transfer to monophonic, gamaka-heavy Carnatic melody.

The PRD set out five goals, three non-negotiable success metrics, and an explicit non-goal list. The primary goals: **G1** composition ID ≥70% top-1 on a held-out set despite artist/tempo/shruti variation; **G2** raga classification ≥75% across ~60+ Saraga-covered ragas; **G3** tala (rhythmic cycle) detection; **G4** lyrics + coherent English meaning; **G5** a working Python MVP. Explicit non-goals for the MVP: word-level synced lyrics, the full theoretical universe of ragas, out-of-catalog composition identification, a native iOS app, and Hindustani (North Indian classical) music support.

Four user personas were defined: Deepti herself (builder + primary user), the concert-goer, the student, and a "technical evaluator" persona (an admissions reader, science-fair judge, or MIR researcher) — a detail that turned out to matter, since the project's rigorous audit culture reads very much like something built to survive exactly that kind of scrutiny.

The planned architecture (Essentia/librosa for pitch + tonic extraction, PyTorch/TensorFlow CNN or TDNN for raga, DTW for composition matching, karnatik.com + an LLM for lyrics/meaning, Streamlit for the MVP UI) mapped closely, at least at first, onto what was actually built — though nearly every specific modeling choice in it (DTW composition matching, CNN raga classification, unsupervised tala detection) would later be tried, measured, and abandoned. The PRD's own risk table anticipated some of this: it flagged "Western chroma features transfer poorly" as a *certain, critical-impact* risk and correctly prescribed tonic-normalized pitch contours instead — advice the project followed from day one and never had to relitigate. It also flagged the Saraga dataset's CC-BY-NC-SA license as a blocker for any future commercial App Store release, a legal question that remains open and unresolved throughout the whole two-month history captured here.

An eight-week, ~85–105 hour timeline was estimated. In practice the project ran far longer than eight weeks and consumed vastly more effort than estimated, but its shape — MVP first, then raga, then composition matching, then lyrics, then generalization testing — is roughly the order things actually happened in, for the first few weeks at least.

## 5. Phase I — Scaffolding, MVP, and the first raga classifier (June 24 – June 30)

The repository opens on 2026-06-24 with the PRD, agent-role definitions, and basic project scaffolding (`pyproject.toml`, directory layout, shared schemas). Within a day, core ML modules land: a raga classifier, tala detector, DTW-based composition matcher, and a lyrics pipeline. By June 25 there's a working Streamlit MVP that runs the whole pipeline end-to-end locally.

June 26 improves the raga classifier with bigram/trigram features and a `predict_raga()` entry point. Then a several-day gap until June 29, when the project's shape changes significantly: a real website is added (Next.js frontend + FastAPI backend), along with a concert-audio scraper and a raga-name normalizer, and the meaning-generation LLM is swapped from Anthropic's API to Gemini 2.5 Flash (evidently a cost decision — this recurs as a theme, with the project operating under tight or zero API budgets for most of its life).

**June 30 is the single busiest day of the whole project (15 commits)**: mic-recording UI, a full `POST /predict-audio` pipeline wired to real audio, Demucs (`htdemucs`) vocal separation added and then debugged through several iterations (a `--model` vs `-n` flag bug, a `torch==2.5.1`/`torchaudio==2.5.1` pin needed to fix audio saving, temp-file writing so ffmpeg could decode WebM/M4A/OGG from the browser), and backend/frontend hardening (rate limiting, a Demucs concurrency semaphore, CORS, content-type allowlisting, fetch timeouts, a Safari MIME fix, a cold-start message for the free-tier host). By day's end the live-microphone pipeline described in the current `README.md` — MediaRecorder → `/predict-audio` → Demucs → `librosa.pyin` pitch extraction → tonic as median voiced F0 → 480-dim tonic-normalized histogram + bigram/trigram features → RandomForest (raga) + L2-distance catalog lookup (composition), run concurrently — was essentially in place, and remains architecturally the "legacy" path still live in production as of this writing.

By the time `HANDOFF_RAGA_DATA.md` was written (July 7, but describing state as of roughly this period), the production raga classifier was live at **40.5% top-1 / 60.8% top-3 across 53 ragas**, and composition matching (L2 distance over 500-point tonic-normalized resampled pitch contours) was live at roughly **16% top-1** — both numbers that the current README still cites as the honest state of the *deployed legacy* system, months later.

## 6. Phase II — Raga data scaling and the CNN detour (July 1 – July 8)

Early July was spent trying to close the raga-accuracy gap through more data and a better model. `HANDOFF_RAGA_DATA.md` (July 7) documents the key empirical finding that shaped the rest of the raga workstream: **accuracy is almost a linear function of tracks-per-raga**. At ≥5 tracks/raga (52 ragas, 507 tracks), top-1 was 36.9%; at ≥8 tracks, 43.2%; at ≥10, 49.1%; at ≥15, 64.8%; at ≥20, 73.3%. The corpus at the time stood at 597 tracks across 88 raga labels, with a median of only ~5 tracks/raga — meaning most ragas were nowhere near the accuracy-favorable end of that curve.

A parallel attempt to replace the RandomForest with a from-scratch CNN on log-CQT features, trained on Colab GPU, failed twice: with heavy regularization, results were chance-level; with lighter regularization, training accuracy crawled to 13% while validation pinned at exact chance (2.2%) for 60 straight epochs. The diagnosis, confirmed with a sanity/overfit check, was pure per-recording memorization with zero cross-track transfer — roughly 29 minutes of audio per raga class simply cannot train a from-scratch CNN across 53 classes. This closed the "better model" branch of the raga problem for the rest of the project's life: every subsequent raga improvement (see §14) came from more/wider data or better feature fusion, never a new model architecture.

Tala detection was also closed in this period at 16.5% top-1 versus a 72% majority-class baseline (always guessing the most common tala, Ādi) — concluded not viable with unsupervised beat-tracking and cycle-periodicity approaches, and excluded from the API and frontend from that point on. It never reopens anywhere in the project's later history.

Tonic (shruti) estimation was fixed on July 2 by switching from a naive median-F0 estimate to Essentia's drone-based `TonicIndianArtMusic`, a change that stuck for the rest of the project, though — as later chapters show — tonic estimation on *wild* (non-drone) audio remained an unsolved, if ultimately low-stakes, problem throughout.

The README was rewritten on July 8 "to describe the actual project (was a 2-line placeholder)" — the version quoted in §1 above descends from this rewrite, kept current ever since.

## 7. Phase III — v3 raga, Qmax composition matching, and the pivot to clip ID (July 9 – July 12)

July 9 introduced two things that would dominate — and eventually be retracted from — the project's story: a **v3 raga model** using full-track TDMS (time-delayed melody surface) + histogram fusion, claimed at **72.8% top-1** on an 18-raga, ≥25-tracks/raga tier; and **Qmax**, a cover-song-similarity-based composition matcher, initially claimed around **60–67% top-1** on full recordings. A "SoundHound-mode" short-clip composition evaluation was also run this day, reporting 55% top-1 on 60-second clips — a number that would later be identified as the origin of the project's foundational "same-corpus benchmarks lie" lesson, since it was flattered by artist/recording-family overlap between train and test.

**July 11–12 is the project's real turning point.** In an all-night session ("after two days of continuous work"), Deepti reframed the whole product: not a research pipeline, but explicitly **"SoundHound for Carnatic music, as a serious product — potentially a company."** The target: point a phone at a performance (or sing/hum) and get composition ID (the headline feature, ≥50% acceptable on wild ~60s clips with a top-5 UX), raga (secondary, always honestly labeled low-confidence), and lyrics + meaning.

This session produced the project's single most important methodological discovery, restated in nearly every subsequent document: **"benchmarks lie, wild clips don't."** Deepti personally recorded 10 real wild clips from actual performances (the seed of what became `~/sung_tests`, with ground truth encoded directly in filenames as `<title>__<raga>.m4a`), and scored the existing system against them. The results were sobering and immediately reshaped priorities: the **lyrics path** (Whisper `large-v3-turbo` + fuzzy token-coverage matching against a composition registry) scored 2/8 top-1, 6/8 top-5 and was declared the winner, ship-grade with a top-5 UI; the **melody path (Qmax)** scored **0/8–10 regardless of variant** — dead on wild clips despite its strong full-recording numbers; and the **raga TDMS clip classifier** scored 0/10 top-1, 1/10 top-3 — weak on wild clips.

A long graveyard of dead melody-path approaches accumulated in this session and is carried forward almost verbatim into every later architecture doc: global-contour DTW (16–20% same-corpus, 0% wild), melodic n-grams and Smith-Waterman (≤10%), Qmax (0% wild), raga-gating of composition candidates (anti-informative — wrong tonics score higher due to class priors), and wild-clip tonic estimation (scattering 158–371 Hz on same-shruti material).

Through the rest of July 11–12, `identify_clip.py` was built up iteratively and scored repeatedly against the growing wild-clip set: a turbo-only prototype (2/10 top-1, 6/10 top-5, with bluffs), then dual-ASR + a "policy v2" selection scheme (3/10, zero bluffs), then a rebuilt composition registry (3,638 raw titles collapsed into 2,231 canonical compositions across 755 alias groups) plus a quality tie-break, reaching **4/10 top-1, 6/10 top-5, zero bluffs** — declared "ship-grade" and staged (not yet deployed) as a FastAPI `/identify` + `/feedback` backend, gated on Deepti's sign-off. A GPU-only experimental variant (large-v3 Whisper on Demucs stems) reached 5/10 top-1, 7/10 top-5, but wasn't freely servable. Notably, one clip recorded with a real tanbura drone got its raga correct for the first time ever, attributed to the drone letting tonic estimation lock correctly — the strongest evidence in the whole project that tonic, not melody, was the raga path's real weak point on wild audio.

`BRAINSTORM_COMPANY.md` (July 11) laid out the strategic framing that persisted for the rest of the project: **the product is the canonical composition registry + rendition/query graph, not the models** — models are replaceable, verified Carnatic wild-clip-quality data is not available anywhere else. Every subsequent architectural decision (user-confirm feedback flywheel, confidence-aware abstention UX, a clip-type router distinguishing kriti/alapana/thani/viruttam, hybrid retrieval with per-source calibration) traces back to this framing.

## 8. Phase IV — Wild-set growth, the zero-cost window, and CoverHunter (July 17 – July 20)

Deepti spent the intervening days recording and fetching more labeled wild clips per the previous session's mandate ("do NOT tune on the current 10"). By **July 18**, the wild set stood at **63 clips (36 in-catalog + 27 out-of-catalog/OOC)**, explicitly "frozen as baseline" — meaning tuning was now finally permitted, on the condition that every change be re-scored and kept or reverted strictly on evidence. The frozen-baseline scoreboard: composition top-1 8/36, top-5 13/36; OOC reject 21/27 (6 bluffs); raga top-1/top-3 9/43/12/43. Several infrastructure bugs in the clip-fetching script were found and fixed (a `mktemp` bug that silently failed every download; macOS `mktemp` not substituting mid-filename; `ffmpeg`/`yt-dlp` eating heredoc stdin and truncating titles). A queued fix — adding Kannada to Whisper's language list, to help a batch of ASR-dead dasa-kriti clips — was tried and explicitly *reverted* the next session when it made things worse, an early instance of the project's discipline of not keeping changes that don't measurably help.

**July 19** introduced a new hard constraint that shaped the rest of the summer: a **zero-cost window** (no paid APIs, gated behind an explicit `CARNATIFY_BILLING_OK=1` environment flag settable only by Deepti), imposed for roughly 30 days. Under this constraint, a synthetic-clip-augmentation pipeline (`augment_wild.py`: pitch shift, time-stretch, reverb, noise, phone-bandpass/mu-law simulation) was built to generate 80 synthetic clips and a 500-clip synthetic training suite, and a Claude Haiku 4.5 meanings-generation batch (~$3.54 estimated cost) was staged but never submitted — Deepti explicitly declined the spend on July 19, and the project's `OPEN_DECISIONS.md` still lists it as "stays UNSUBMITTED... do not re-ask soon" as of late July. A control experiment this session accidentally discovered something important: a "0/80" synthetic-suite score under a fast (no-Demucs) configuration turned out to also apply to 10 *unaugmented* control clips (0/10, all ASR-dead) — proving Demucs vocal separation was load-bearing for the whole pipeline, not that augmentation itself had broken anything. When properly isolated, augmentation was found to genuinely damage raga accuracy (6/10 clean → 12/77 ≈16% augmented) and to push Whisper into fluent hallucination that defeated the usability gates entirely.

Given a choice between rerunning the synthetic suite overnight (Option A) or spending local cycles growing the real wild set (Option B), Deepti chose B. **Session 0719B** executed on that: the wild-clip fetcher was hardened with real validation (ffmpeg integrity checks, a 58-second duration floor, silence/volume checks, diacritic-insensitive deduplication), growing the set from **63 to 106 clips (78 in-catalog + 28 OOC)**. In parallel, **CoverHunter** — an unmaintained open-source melodic cover-song-identification model — was set up on Colab and debugged through a long chain of failures: wrong config keys, a `typeguard` version incompatibility, a silently-empty validation split from splitting on the wrong ID field, and — most consequentially — a bug in the upstream `tools/train.py` that unpacked two values from a three-value return, silently killing every training run after epoch 1 and invalidating every mAP number measured against it up to that point. Colab VM deaths and a broken resume mechanism (silently restarting training from scratch) compounded the debugging. First reference numbers logged (mAP 0.047–0.078, hit@1 0.08–0.14 against ~0.036 chance) were judged "above chance," with a stated bar of mAP 0.3–0.5 needed to justify further investment — a bar CoverHunter would fail decisively five days later (§10).

## 9. Phase V — The multi-agent protocol is formalized; Phase 0; the Rung ladder (July 21 – July 23)

**July 21** is where the project's governing structure snaps into its final shape. The Fable/Codex relay is formalized explicitly by name in `HANDOFF_SESSION_0721.md`, `AGENT_LOG.md` is created as the shared coordination channel, and the wild set is re-baselined for the first time on the full 106 clips: composition top-1 11/78, top-5 18/78; OOC reject 24/28 (4 bluffs); raga top-1/top-3 18/85/21/85. Crucially, the original curated-36 subset reproduced exactly its earlier 8/36 score — confirming there was no code regression, and that the apparent drop from 22% to 14% top-1 was purely a *composition* effect: the newly auto-fetched clips were 64% ASR-dead, far worse than the roughly one-third the strategy docs had assumed.

Codex's first cold-read code review (31KB, `CODEX_REVIEW.md`) surfaced several real defects that Fable independently verified line-by-line: a `fold()` function that stripped all non-ASCII characters via NFKD normalization before matching, silently destroying every Indic-script (Tamil/Telugu/Hindi) Whisper hypothesis; a policy divergence where the staged backend lacked the repetition-loop hallucination gate present in the main script; a "many-to-one" scoring exploit letting repeated tokens (e.g., "rama rama rama") inflate match scores with no real information; a "longest transcript wins" heuristic that discarded language/likelihood signal; and a cache keyed only by filename, with no audio hash, no atomicity, and thus no protection against poisoning.

A strategic reframe was accepted the same day and is still in force as of the most recent handoff: the project's primary metric became **precision at coverage** (target ≥85% precision among *answered* clips, published alongside raw top-1/top-5/OOC numbers so abstention can't be gamed) rather than the PRD's original "80-90% top-1." This is formalized in `METRIC_CONTRACT.md`, adopted 2026-07-21: the reasoning is that an arbitrary 60-second window can contain alapana, thani, tuning, applause, or silence — with no composition-identifying evidence for even an expert listener — so treating every window as a mandatory prediction creates an irreducible error floor and rewards bluffing. The defensible product is *selective*: answer when the evidence supports one work, abstain with an explanation otherwise.

"Phase 0" (correctness/observability fixes only, no new features) was greenlit under a strict one-change-one-eval discipline, with the original curated-36 clips as a hard regression floor (must never drop below 8/36). It landed as six separately-committed, individually-revertible fixes: the Unicode `fold()` bug, the scoring exploit, a shared policy function used by both the script and the staged backend, a versioned (v2) audio-hash-keyed cache, stripped synthetic-clip contamination from the legacy caches, and general hygiene. The blessed post-Phase-0 baseline: **composition top-1 14/78, top-5 23/78, OOC reject 21/28 (7 bluffs)**, curated-36 guard 9/36. A full re-transcription of all 106 clips under the new cache (17.3 hours of CPU time) revealed that 585 hypotheses had actually contained usable native Indic script that the old `fold()` bug had erased entirely — a striking illustration of how much signal one small Unicode bug had been silently discarding.

**July 22** produced the project's most consequential single diagnostic: **the 10/1/1 recall decomposition.** Of 22 ASR-usable auto-fetched clips, the true composition appeared in the matcher's own top-100 candidate list for only 10 of them — the other 12 truths were entirely absent, no matter how the reranker was tuned. Of those 12 misses: 10 were ASR-dead (9 fluent English hallucination, 1 a wordless svara passage with genuinely no lyrics to recover); 1 had correct ASR but zero linked lyric lines in the catalog — a *catalog coverage* gap, not an ASR failure; and only 1 was actually recoverable by improving the matcher. This reframed the whole bottleneck: **the matcher was not the constraint. Catalog coverage and ASR quality were.** An "accepted ladder" of five numbered "Rungs" was defined the same day: Rung 1 (an exact, reviewed truth manifest, replacing a fuzzy matcher that had been quietly inflating scores — one specific bonus find was that the generic title "rAma nee" had been fuzzy-matching 70 unrelated registry rows) and Rung 2 (lyric-link coverage overrides) went to Opus; Rung 3 (a from-scratch IndicConformer ASR integration, described as "the one brutal task") to Fable; Rungs 4–5 later.

**July 23** executed Rungs 3–5. Rung 3 integrated AI4Bharat's IndicConformer-600M multilingual ONNX CTC model as a wholly separate ASR channel — dramatically faster than Whisper (8.7 minutes for all 106 clips versus Whisper's 17.3 hours) and able to recover real Carnatic sahitya where Whisper hallucinated fluent English, but with precision that collapsed on its own (27 of 28 OOC clips falsely answered when used alone). Codex specified a precise arbitration rule for **Rung 4**: score Whisper and IndicConformer as independent channels; IndicConformer is only eligible to override Whisper if a coverage feature `q_cov` (share of the query's IDF-weighted vocabulary explained by the candidate match) clears 0.250 *and* the match is token-ordered; when both channels qualify, Whisper always wins. This shipped as an experimental opt-in backend at 11/78 top-1, 25/78 top-5, 10 OOC bluffs, 20% precision at 52% coverage — a real improvement, but nowhere near the 85% precision bar, so it never became the default. **Rung 5** added a third lane, IndicConformer running on the Demucs-separated vocal stem rather than the mix, motivated by evidence that stems accounted for most of Whisper's correct answers; it initially looked sideways versus Rung 4 until two genuine registry "work-family" duplicates (the same composition catalogued as multiple distinct entries) were found and corrected, at which point it became a clean, strict improvement: 13/78 top-1, 28/78 top-5, 23% precision at 53% coverage.

The same day, a precision-ceiling diagnosis closed off further tuning of the whole ASR-matcher family: sweeping every confidence/margin/tier threshold never exceeded roughly 31% precision anywhere, and — more damning — when Whisper and IndicConformer independently *agreed* on an answer, that agreement was only 40–43% correct, because their errors were correlated (both models mishear the same ornamented sung audio the same way, rather than making independent mistakes that cross-validate each other). The stated verdict: the shared bottleneck was that **speech-ASR fundamentally cannot read sung Carnatic audio reliably**, and every downstream improvement was being starved by garbage lyrics regardless of how the matcher was tuned. The only remaining lever identified was domain-adapted ASR fine-tuning.

## 10. Phase VI — The ASR ceiling is confirmed dead; melody dies too; the pivot to duration (July 24 – 25)

**July 24** tested the fine-tuning lever directly and killed it decisively. A LoRA fine-tune of Whisper-small was trained on ~80 minutes of weakly-aligned Saraga pallavi audio (249 tracks, 354 segments, cleaned of embedded notation markup) and made the pipeline **strictly worse** — composition top-1 collapsed from the 8/78 stock baseline to 0–1/78, because the adapter had overfit hard on roughly 26 memorized pallavis and began emitting their tokens regardless of the actual input audio. The verdict was unambiguous: "Stock Whisper's 8/78 top-1 is the realistic ceiling of lyrics-ASR here. Stop investing in transcribe-then-match." The same day, the roster changed again: "Fable" was formally retired (its API budget essentially exhausted), handing off via a lengthy document (`HANDOFF_WORKHORSE_FABLE_TO_OPUS5.md`) to a new "Opus 5 workhorse," alongside a renamed "Workhorse GPT-5.6" continuing Codex's role.

The same session also closed the melodic identification channel. A rigorous source-disjoint re-score of CoverHunter found clean mAP of **0.113** on a 137-clip set — versus a contaminated dev-set headline of 0.327 that had been the basis for cautious optimism five days earlier. Roughly two-thirds of the original number turned out to be same-recording contamination (a crop of the same source track sitting in both the query and reference sets). The signal was real (3.6× chance) but far short of the ~0.30 bar the project had set in advance to justify further investment. This closed the melodic-identification door alongside the already-dead lyrics-ASR door — both of the project's two hoped-for identification channels were now measured dead within a single 24-hour span.

**July 25 is the project's second major pivot session.** With both cheap identification levers exhausted, Deepti ran a full strategy re-think with the verifying agent and produced a new hypothesis that reframed everything that followed: **every measured composition/melody failure had been on 30–60 second clips, while the same underlying feature families scored 60–72% on full recordings** (referring to the then-current raga and Qmax full-track numbers). The product's assumed "Shazam-style 30-second input contract" was never actually load-bearing for the use case — Carnatic pieces run 10–30 minutes and a listener is physically present the whole time. Deepti set a new concrete target: **the accuracy of a 5-minute listen, delivered in 90 seconds or less.** Composer identification and tala prediction were formally parked as non-goals from this point forward. Jon (§4) was introduced as the design reference user in this same session.

An immediate rigorous **duration probe** protocol was specified: cut existing multi-rendition concert audio into 30s/1min/3min/5min windows with *randomized*, not fixed/prefix-anchored, start offsets (a fixed start was explicitly flagged as biased against short windows, since Carnatic concert openings are typically lyric-free alapana), scored on the lyrics/Whisper channel only, with a pre-registered promotion gate requiring a ≥15-percentage-point absolute gain in top-5 from 30s to 5min, monotone across all four durations. The same session, CoverHunter's melodic channel received its own formal Stage-1 go/no-go and also failed, at a clean source-disjoint mAP of **0.113** against a 0.031 chance floor — closing "the last identification channel that could have been fixed without new data" and formally ruling out any further narrowing of the product toward composition-only ID.

## 11. Phase VII — The Duration Probe: three red-team rounds, a rebuilt negative arm, and a clean KILL (July 25 – July 30)

What followed was the most methodologically elaborate single experiment in the project's history, and it is worth walking through in some detail because its discipline became the template for everything that came after (§14).

**Round 1 red team (Codex, `CODEX_DURATION_PROBE_REVIEW.md`, ~July 25):** delivered a hard "do not transcribe" verdict before any GPU time was spent, finding: the candidate pool wasn't correctly deduplicated by registry ID (inflating an "ambiguous" bucket to 254 titles); the statistical test computing the confidence interval was actually the ordinary standard error of a single rate, not of the *paired* difference the design claimed to measure, discarding the pairing structure entirely; strict monotonicity as a second gate condition was shown to be a nearly useless filter (only ~4% of orderings of four exchangeable points are monotone by chance); the primary sampling scheme drew *independent* random offsets per duration for the same title, meaning the 30s and 300s "windows" for one title could come from completely unrelated parts of the recording rather than measuring progressive listening from one start; there was no 90-second condition despite 90 seconds being the actual product target; and there was no negative/safety arm at all, so the design couldn't distinguish "duration helps" from "duration also makes the system bluff more."

The design was rebuilt and frozen in `DURATION_PROBE_PREREGISTRATION.md` (July 26, amended July 28): five durations (30/60/90/180/300s) nested from one random anchor per title (true progressive listening), a three-way decision rule (PROMOTE / KILL / INCONCLUSIVE, replacing the old binary gate that had no room for a legitimately inconclusive result), and the frozen 7-pass production ASR configuration.

**July 28 — the negative arm is found invalid.** Multiple further defects surfaced, mostly via a second Codex red-team pass: a title-extraction bug let numeric archive IDs leak into supposedly out-of-catalog "titles," and at least three of twelve nominal OOC items turned out to be demonstrably *in-catalog* on manual resolution; the twelve "independent" OOC sources actually traced back to only three underlying archive uploads, overstating diversity; statistical power at the achieved sample size was far too low (as low as 2.4% in the worst stratum) to support any safety claim at all. A subtler, genuinely interesting musical finding emerged in the same review: both labeling sheets had filed bare *swaram* (solfège syllables) under "no lyrics" — but in real Carnatic performance practice, *swarakalpana* alternates solfège with the actual sung lyric line in call-and-response, so audio a labeler flags as "has swara in it" typically *does* contain real words. Deepti's own ear-labeling (she wrote "has swara in it" while still labeling one item as "has sahitya") caught this before the sheet's flawed category definitions could poison the results — a concrete instance of a lesson the project restates often: a category a musician would catch and an engineer wouldn't is exactly what an ear-labeling sheet exists to catch, and its definitions need review by the ear before it ships, not after.

**July 29 — the negative arm is rebuilt.** Title extraction was repaired and verified to be a true no-op against the already-correct portion of the pool. A dual resolution-key system was introduced: an exact key as the only route into "truth," and a deliberately looser key that could only ever be used to *disqualify* a candidate, never to create one — trading some sample size for guaranteed conservatism. The rebuilt arm covered only the 30s and 300s endpoints (dropping the three middle durations to roughly double the number of independent items at the same GPU cost) and, most strikingly, found that **the OOC stratum came back completely empty**: of 1,238 recordings on disk, 1,175 resolved cleanly to known works, and none of the remaining 11 unresolved recordings named an identifiable composition at all (they were upload IDs, catalogue numbers, archive slot names). The document concluded flatly that "OOC abstention at length is unmeasurable from assets on disk" — the project's entire existing reviewed OOC clip set topped out at 84 seconds, far short of the 300-second endpoint the probe needed, and acquiring new long-form OOC audio was explicitly left as Deepti's call to fund or not.

A second Codex red-team pass on the rebuild (`CODEX_NEGATIVE_ARM_REVIEW_R2.md`) made one especially sharp and, crucially, later *empirically confirmed* prediction: the negative arm's single listening sweep, despite covering 50% of each window's duration with a maximum 10-second gap, could not actually support the claim "no lyrics anywhere in this 300-second window," because a short pallavi phrase hiding in an unheard 10-second gap would be enough to fool the matcher. A repair built a **complementary sweep** covering exactly the seconds the first sweep never played, combined under the rule that singing heard on *either* sweep settles an item as sung. When Deepti completed the second labeling pass, two items (`neg053`, `neg020`) were found to carry singing entirely inside the gap the first sweep had missed — a measured **4% single-sweep contamination rate**, vindicating the red-team's theoretical concern with a real, counted number.

**July 30 — the run, and the result.** The actual GPU transcription run, executed by Deepti across multiple Colab sessions on shifting hardware (a T4, then a faster unrecorded GPU, then an A100), immediately produced the exact kind of provenance problem the project's own "graveyard of process errors" had warned against: the runner script wrote its device/provenance log only *after* completing its final processing step, and one session died mid-run on a missing `rapidfuzz` import after roughly 27 GPU-hours of work, losing the device map entirely. A forensic device-calibration protocol was pre-frozen (before any result was read) specifying exactly how to handle straddler windows that might have crossed hardware mid-title; ultimately one title (`probe0051`) was confirmed as a genuine cross-device straddler and excluded.

**The result: a clean, pre-registered KILL.** Paired top-5 accuracy on the lyrics/Whisper channel moved from **5.1% at 30 seconds to 1.7% at 300 seconds — a decline of 3.4 percentage points**, with a 90% confidence interval of [−8.9pp, +1.8pp] sitting entirely below the +15pp promotion threshold. Longer listening windows made the lyrics-ASR matcher *worse*, not better. The negative/safety arm returned "NOT ESTABLISHED" (underpowered, not evidence of a rise) rather than either SAFE or a rise — an honest, if unsatisfying, non-conclusion given the sample size actually achieved. The handoff for this session explicitly calls the KILL "the strongest-evidenced result in the repository: pre-registered, three red-team passes, scored exactly once," and closes the loop that started the whole duration-probe workstream: duration is retired as a lever *for the lyrics-ASR channel specifically*, and the earlier working hypothesis that duration alone explained the gap between full-recording and wild-clip numbers is formally retracted.

## 12. Phase VIII — Contamination audits: Qmax retracted, raga survives (July 30 – 31)

With the duration probe closed, the project turned its now-sharpened audit discipline on its own remaining positive claims — first Qmax, then raga.

**Qmax.** `WORKHORSE_QMAX_AUDIT_BRIEF.md` (July 30) targeted "the last surviving positive result in the repository," the ~60–67% full-recording Qmax composition-matching claim. `data/QMAX_CONTAMINATION_AUDIT.md` reconstructed the original evaluation protocol and re-scored it at three levels of increasing disjointness. The result was severe: **the external-arm claim collapsed from 60.0% to 9.5% source-disjoint.** Of the top-1 hits the original evaluation had counted as correct, **34 of 36 were byte-identical copies of the query recording** — sitting at the exact numerical floor for an identical sequence — meaning the "match" was the query finding a literal or near-literal copy of itself in the reference catalog, not genuine cross-rendition composition identification. A separate internal 66.7% claim (n=30) was found to be real but unreproducible (its underlying feature cache had never been committed). Most damningly, the audit found the corpus simply cannot support a genuine cross-artist claim at any confidence level: **only 13 of 1,011 works have two or more known distinct performing artists.** Qmax composition matching, the project's oldest positive result, was retracted outright.

**Raga.** The same audit methodology, applied the same day to the standing "72.8% top-1 / 84.7% top-3" full-track raga claim, found the *opposite* result: it survived essentially unchanged. The historical evaluation harness (rediscovered, after some searching, in a scratch-archive script) reproduced the original number on the exact same integer counts, and held at **72.2% top-1 / 84.0% top-3 source-disjoint** even after enforcing full independence between train and test recording sources. The predicted contamination mechanism (same-raga, same-upload clusters) was confirmed to exist, but it cost only **1.2 percentage points** — the opposite of Qmax's near-total collapse. One weak class was flagged: Vasanta, at 35.7% top-1, a number that would recur consistently at almost every later re-measurement in this series regardless of duration, degradation, or class-tier width.

This pair of results — one channel's headline number destroyed, the other's confirmed genuine — reframed the whole rest of the project. Composition identification was now understood to have no surviving positive result anywhere (every one of five identification channels was measured dead: lyrics-ASR precision, sung-ASR fine-tuning, melodic CSI, duration, and now Qmax). Raga classification, on the other hand, was the one channel that had survived every audit thrown at it, which is what motivated the entire deep investigation described next.

**July 31 continued the raga audit into a duration decomposition** (isolating how much of the 72.2%-full-track-to-0/10-wild-clip gap was explained by *shorter* audio, as opposed to *field/real-world* audio conditions): 52.5% at 30s → 60.4% at 60s → 63.8% at 120s → 68.8% at 300s → 71.0% at 600s → 72.2% full-track. The gap versus full-track was statistically real at 30/60/120 seconds but not distinguishable from zero at 300/600 seconds — the "knee" of the curve sat between 120 and 300 seconds. Retraining directly on short windows helped somewhat but didn't close the gap to full-track levels, meaning most of the shortfall at short durations reflects information genuinely absent from a short clip, not merely a model that hadn't seen short examples during training. **Duration, on its own, cost about 12 percentage points** (72.2% → 60.4% at 60s) — much less than the roughly 44-point gap the project had been assuming existed between clean full-recording numbers and the historical wild-clip failure.

## 13. Phase IX — Domain decomposition and the first real wild-clip raga measurement (July 31 – August 1)

If duration only cost ~12 points, something else had to explain the rest of the historical gap to a 0/10 wild-clip score. A domain-decomposition experiment isolated exactly which components of "real-world" degradation were responsible, by testing each individually against the historical `augment_wild.py` pipeline, which the project realized (for the first time, explicitly, this session) had been bundling two categorically different kinds of transformation together: three that genuinely simulate what happens to a real phone recording (pink noise, room reverb, phone-mic bandpass filtering), and two — pitch-shift and time-stretch — that were originally added purely as training-time invariance tricks and **cannot happen to an actual recording of a real performance**.

The pre-registered hypothesis (that a phone-mic high-pass filter would strip the vocal fundamental and destroy melody tracking outright) was cleanly **refuted** — zero contour failures occurred across 517 test windows in every condition. The headline finding: **field-realistic degradation (phone chain + room reverb + background noise, combined) costs only about 7.5 percentage points, not the ~44 points the project had feared.** Each individual field-realistic component's confidence interval touched or crossed zero on its own. By contrast, the artificial pitch-shift and time-stretch components — which don't occur in real recordings — were each *individually* more damaging (10.6 and 11.0 points respectively) than all three real field components combined, meaning the historically-quoted "16% under augmentation" figure had been dominated by artifacts of the *simulation*, not by anything a real phone microphone actually does. This also killed a long-standing hypothesis that tonic (pitch-reference) estimation was the raga path's central bottleneck: contour damage outweighed tonic damage by 3-to-1 in the realistic condition and 5-to-1 in the worst condition, and perfect tonic recovery was calculated to be worth only about 2.2 additional points.

With both duration and domain now bounded and found to be smaller problems than assumed, the project ran the actual missing experiment: a genuine field measurement on real wild audio. `WORKHORSE_RAGA_WILD_BRIEF.md` (July 31) commissioned a frozen, hash-verified set of **29 wild clips** whose filename-derived raga label fell within the model's then-current 18-class vocabulary, committed with per-clip SHA-256 hashes *before* any prediction was generated. Verification confirmed zero training-audio overlap (checked by hash against all 2,229 training files). The result: **16/29 = 55.2% top-1**, 95% confidence interval [37.5%, 71.6%] — comfortably excluding both the 5.6% pure-chance floor and a 17.2% best-constant-guess floor, and comfortably containing an earlier simulated estimate of 48.7%. Top-3 was 19–22 out of 29 depending on scope. **This flatly refuted the years-old "raga dies on wild audio" pessimism** that had held since the project's very first 0/10 wild-clip result back on July 11. That original 0/10 was retroactively explained (with an initial over-claim that was itself corrected the next day) as an artifact of a completely different, older model using a documented-bad tonic convention, tested on clips whose true ragas were mostly outside its vocabulary in the first place — not evidence that the *current* raga approach fails in the field.

Two weeks later, on August 13, Deepti personally reviewed all 29 wild-clip labels by ear — flagged in the original brief as "the highest value-per-minute action available" — and corrected two: one clip mislabeled Sāvēri that was actually Ābhēri, and one mislabeled Kalyāṇi that was actually Yamunākalyāṇi, neither of which the model could name at all, so both became ineligible rather than counted as misses. This had been anticipated in advance (the original document had already published this exact 27-clip sensitivity), so the correction selected between two pre-published branches rather than creating a new, potentially self-serving one. The corrected headline: **16/27 = 59.3% top-1** [40.7%, 75.5%], **20/27 = 74.1% top-3** — the number quoted as current throughout the rest of the project.

## 14. Phase X — The raga ceiling investigation: vocabulary, coverage, and the tier graduation (August 13)

By early August, the picture for raga classification was clear on every axis except one: duration was priced (~12pp cost), domain was priced (~7.5pp cost, approximately nothing once wild-clip evidence arrived), contamination was ruled out (72.2% held), and the field number (59.3%) was statistically indistinguishable from the clean-corpus number at the same duration (60.4% at 60 seconds) — meaning, in the project's own words, **"the whole gap between 72.2% full-track and 59.3% wild is duration, not domain."** What remained unexplained was why the classifier still seemed capped well below its full-track ceiling in any practical setting, and on **August 13** the project produced its most consequential single-day body of work to answer that.

**The reframe (`data/RAGA_CEILING_EVALUATION.md`):** the constraint was never really accuracy — it was **vocabulary**. The classifier could not *name* 40–60% of what a real listener actually hears, because of three stacked, independently-chosen caps: a 40-name reference raga vocabulary (from CompMusic ∪ Saraga) that 274 scraped raga spellings (18.6% of the labeled corpus) had never matched and so were silently dropped to `null`; a hard-coded download target of 30 tracks per raga class, meaning no raga in the corpus could ever exceed that regardless of how much audio actually existed; and `min_tracks`, the class-admission threshold itself, whose accuracy-versus-coverage tradeoff had genuinely never been priced before this day.

**The coverage curve (`data/RAGA_COVERAGE_CURVE.md`):** measured source-disjoint top-1/top-3 at four class-tier widths, holding everything else fixed. Accuracy fell log-linearly with class count (18 classes → 32 → 44 → 54: top-1 72.2% → 64.0% → 59.9% → 57.0%), which sounds like a regression — but repertoire coverage rose far faster (39.1% → 59.6% → 70.9% → 79.2%), so the metric the project decided actually mattered, **expected correct answers per 100 concert items heard (coverage × accuracy)**, rose *monotonically*: 28.2 → 38.2 → 42.5 → 45.1, peaking at the widest tier measured rather than somewhere in the middle as the verifier had predicted in advance. A confound-free "within-class learning curve" (each class compared against itself at capped subsample sizes, controlling for the fact that popular ragas both have more data *and* are inherently easier) confirmed the underlying data lever was real and substantial: **+18.4 percentage points from 10 to 30 tracks per class, with no saturation anywhere in the corpus** — the 30th track bought as much accuracy, on average, as the 10th.

**The tier graduation (`data/RAGA_TIER_GRADUATION.md`):** executed the retrain the coverage curve argued for. After resolving a subtle harness discrepancy (the fused-model evaluation harness replicates each track into three augmented rows, shifting fold boundaries and worth a consistent, now-quantified "+0.35pp noise floor" versus the single-row harness used elsewhere — the two were shown to agree exactly once this was accounted for), the decisive comparison found that **fusing the TDMS melodic feature with the older 480-dim pitch-histogram feature helps more, not less, at wider class tiers** (+1.16pp at 18 classes, growing to +1.21pp at 32, and +1.84pp at 54 classes, p=0.0011) — the opposite of what had been predicted going in, with the largest gains landing precisely on gamaka-and-phrase-heavy classes (Hindōḷaṁ +12.5pp, Śrīranjani +9.5pp) that the original mechanism said fusion should be useless on. A previously-planned narrow class-drop (removing three chronically weak classes, Nāṭakurinji/Janaranjani/Haṁsānandi) was tested directly and found to flip sign between the TDMS-only and fused model arms, clearing significance in neither — so, following a tie-breaking rule written down *before* this result existed ("ties go to admitting everything, since a drop that doesn't clearly pay forfeits repertoire mass for nothing"), **all 54 classes were admitted.**

**The result, exported the same day as `models/raga_v4_*`:** a fused RandomForest model, `min_tracks=10`, **54 raga classes**, admitting every class with no per-class threshold (a separate experiment confirmed thresholding always *loses* expected hits, monotonically, at every level tested). Measured performance, source-disjoint: **top-1 59.2% [55.0, 64.1], top-3 72.8%, repertoire coverage 79.2%, and 46.86 expected correct raga identifications per 100 heard concert items** — a 20% improvement over the 32-class tier the product would ship today if wired in, and a 63% improvement over the original 18-class tier the earliest full-track numbers were measured on.

**The vocabulary audit (`data/RAGA_VOCABULARY_AUDIT.md`, run by Codex in parallel the same day):** independently investigated the 274 "junk" null-mapped raga spellings and found the project's own prior description of them wrong: only 126 (18.3%) were genuinely junk (venue names, dates, artist-name contamination); the remaining 561 of 689 null items (81.4%) were real ragas that had simply never been added to the reference vocabulary at all — the single largest, Ārabhi, accounting for 46 items on its own. Correctly canonicalizing the recoverable names would in principle raise the ceiling to **72 raga classes**, but a follow-up check found this to be a "programme-count ceiling, not an audio ceiling": only 57 of the 561 recoverable entries (10.2%) have any matching cached audio at all, spread thinly across 37 classes with no more than 4 matches each — **zero new classes would actually clear the min-tracks-10 threshold on this recoverable set today.** The audit also surfaced ten cases where the alias map had wrongly *merged* two genuinely different raga names (as opposed to simply missing one), which were repaired the same day with new regression tests, and surfaced several raga-equivalence questions (Ābhēri vs. Karṇāṭaka dēvagāndhāri, Bhīmpalās, Durgā vs. Śuddha Sāvēri) that were explicitly kicked to Deepti as musical judgment calls rather than resolved by the agents (`data/RAGA_MERGE_DECISIONS.md` records her rulings — Ābhēri/Karṇāṭaka dēvagāndhāri are ruled a defensible engineering-level merge though musically distinct; Bhīmpalās is confirmed as the Hindustani form of modern Ābhēri; Durgā and Śuddha Sāvēri are ruled genuinely distinct and must stay separate).

**The fetch recount (`data/RAGA_FETCH_RECOUNT.md`):** closed the loop on whether the data lever could actually be pulled further. Against an earlier optimistic estimate of 1,712 additional fetchable tracks, the real number — computed by running both metadata fetchers to exhaustion against the archive.org cache — was **378 genuinely new (raga, title) pairs, of which only 193 fall within the current 54-class tier: an 11% realized fraction of the earlier ceiling.** Ten of the 54 classes have zero additional reachable audio at all; 41 of 54 have fewer than five additional tracks; only four classes have ten or more. **The metadata itself is exhausted** — every one of 258 previously-uncached identifiers was fetched and returned zero new audio files. Tellingly, the most reachable class, Kalyāṇi (22 available recordings), had already been measured to gain essentially nothing (+0.0pp) from tripled training data, while Bhairavi — which had gained an enormous +50.0pp from tripling — has only three reachable recordings available. The project's own verifier flagged, and then *self-corrected*, an appealing "target the fetch at weak classes" correlation twice in one month, both times identifying it as a regression-to-the-mean statistical artifact once the noisy shared term was removed from both sides of the comparison — a mistake explicitly documented as a lesson (§17).

**State as of August 13–14, 2026 (end of the covered period):** the exported `raga_v4` model (54 classes, fused, 59.2%/72.8% source-disjoint, 79.2% repertoire coverage, 46.86 expected hits/100) exists on disk but, per Deepti's explicit standing directive that day, has **not been wired into `identify_clip.py` or the product** — she asked for the underlying tooling to be made robust first (§6 of `handoff.md`, summarized in §16 below). Also on August 13, Deepti supplied **11 long-form concert recordings from her own equipment (9.85 hours total)**, converted and hash-frozen the same day, explicitly described in the handoff as "the only unmeasured cell in the entire product case" — every wild clip on disk until this point topped out at 85 seconds, while the concert-companion product thesis rests on 10–30 minute items, a duration region measured only on clean archive audio, never on real wild recordings. As of the most recent commit (August 14), labeling of these concerts (one row per musical item, of which each ~30–120 minute concert likely contains 8–15) had not yet been done by Deepti, and no prediction had been run against them — deliberately, since scoring before labels exist was judged to risk building a preregistration around a model tier that might change before the labels landed (which, in fact, it did that same week).

## 15. Architecture: what is live, legacy, and dead

`ARCHITECTURE.md` (written July 20, and still accurate as of the most recent commits) is unusually blunt about this, opening: *"This repo contains three generations of the composition matcher living side by side. Structure alone will mislead you: the dead code is larger, better organized, and more conventionally packaged than the live code, which is one 1,500-line script at the repo root."*

**The live pipeline** is `identify_clip.py`, a standalone root-level script importing nothing from the installed `src/carnatify/` package:

```
audio (~60s wild clip)
  → dual ASR: whisper large-v3-turbo on original audio (langs None/ta/te/hi)
              + on demucs htdemucs vocal stem (None/ta/te)
  → matcher: IDF-weighted token coverage + pallavi-repetition bonus + order
             bonus, over registry title variants AND karnatik lyric-line
             pseudo-variants
  → usability gates: stoplist, min chars, repetition, max-token-run
  → policy v2: best usable variant, prefer stem within 0.15, abstain < 0.35
  → top-5 + confidence tier (high / medium / low / none)
  → raga: melodia → tonic → TDMS → clip RF, always reported low-confidence;
          a confident composition match backfills raga from the registry
          instead (measurably better than the raga model alone)
```

Its only legitimate scoreboard is the wild clip set in `~/sung_tests`; the project's standing rule is that no same-corpus benchmark may green-light anything, a rule "bought with several months of flattered numbers."

**The legacy pipeline**, still literally deployed in production as of this writing, is `backend/main.py`'s older endpoints, serving `carnatify.ml.composition_matcher.match_composition` (~16% composition accuracy) and `carnatify.ml.raga_classifier.predict_raga` (~40.5% raga accuracy) — the same two numbers established back in Phase I/II of this history and never improved in the deployed system, because every subsequent improvement has been staged but never shipped, pending Deepti's sign-off. The staged newer `/identify` + `/feedback` endpoints (built July 11, hardened through Phase 0) exist in the codebase and pass smoke tests but have never been deployed. The deployment script (`backend/build_space.sh`) copies the entire `src/` tree into the production Docker image, graveyard included, which is why the dead modules can't simply be deleted yet — the legacy endpoints still import them.

**The graveyard**, quoting `ARCHITECTURE.md`'s own summary table (each entry backed by a measured, not suspected, result):

| Module / approach | Measured | Verdict |
|---|---|---|
| DTW / contour melody matching | 16–20% same-corpus, 0% on wild 60s clips | dead |

## 16. The data assets — the actual moat

Per `ARCHITECTURE.md`'s own framing, these outlive every model in the repository and are treated as the project's real defensible asset:

- **`data/composition_registry.json`** — 8,688 canonical composition entries with aliases and raga metadata (the canonical registry, expanded from an original 2,231-composition version built July 12).
- **`data/karnatik_lyrics.json`** — scraped lyric pages from karnatik.com, driving line-level (not just title-level) matching.
- **`data/lyrics.db`** — 3,252 composition titles with meanings imported from karnatik.
- **`data/catalog_titles.txt`** — 8,688 grep-able canonical titles; the project's own house rule is to grep this file *before* declaring any clip "out of catalog," after an early embarrassment where 7 of 10 hand-picked "OOC" test clips turned out to already be in the registry.
- **`~/sung_tests/`** — the wild-clip regression set, grown from 10 (July 11) to 63 (July 18) to 106 (July 20) to its current state of 108 files / 106 audio clips / 81 with a real raga label, each with ground truth encoded in the filename. Described repeatedly as "the hardest data class to obtain" and explicitly frozen — its exact composition is load-bearing for numerous committed measurements, and nothing may be silently added to it.
- **`data/concert_audio/`** — roughly 1,238 full-length recordings across 85 raga directories (13GB, gitignored), the source corpus for all full-track raga training and evaluation.
- **`data/scraped_compositions.json`** — 3,698 concert-programme items scraped from a Carnatic-music blog, of which 3,009 carry a canonical raga label; this is the "repertoire prior" that repertoire-coverage percentages (§14) are computed against, chosen deliberately over the model's own training-set distribution because it represents what a concert-companion product would actually need to cover.
- **`data/long_concerts/`** — the 11 concerts Deepti recorded herself (§14), the newest and, per the most recent handoff, single most valuable unexploited asset in the project.

The registry is explicitly known to contain junk rows (addresses and dates mis-parsed as compositions during scraping), and the project's standing discipline is to never trust registry raga metadata without cross-checking it against the separately audited alias file.

## 17. House discipline: how this project keeps itself honest

Because so much of this history is a story of retracted claims, it's worth stating plainly what the project's actual working method became, since that method — not any specific model or number — is arguably the most durable thing it produced. `handoff.md` §8 states this directly as "the part to copy, not the numbers":

- **Preregister, and commit the preregistration as its own commit before the scoring harness exists**, so the ordering lives verifiably in git history rather than in file timestamps that could be edited after the fact.
- **Gate every new measurement on first reproducing a historical number exactly**, on raw integer counts, not rounded percentages — and prefer the strongest form of reproduction available (e.g., requiring a `git diff` against a committed results file to come back completely empty, which proves a refactor is *behavior*-preserving, not merely *number*-preserving).
- **Decide in advance what a gate failure would mean**, so that when one occurs it gets diagnosed rather than silently waved through — one such failure, investigated rather than ignored, produced exact agreement between two previously-suspect measurement harnesses and quantified a general "noise floor" for the whole project.
- **Fix the interpretation table, and any tie-breaking rule, before the number exists** — used explicitly to prevent a near-tied result (the 54-class tier decision) from being read afterward as support for whichever answer would have been more convenient.
- **Write falsifiable predictions down in advance.** By August 13, the project's own running tally stood at **twelve written predictions, later refuted by measurement** — including, notably, several of the verifier's own predictions, not just workhorse agents'.
- **Never sum "produced no answer" with "produced a wrong answer."** Abstention and error are tracked as categorically distinct outcomes throughout, precisely because conflating them is what let the original PRD's "80–90% top-1" target quietly reward bluffing.
- **The verifier distrusts itself as hard as it distrusts a workhorse.** The clearest instance: a "regression to the mean" statistical trap (an apparent correlation between how *weak* a raga class was and how *much* it gained from more data, which evaporates once the shared noisy term is removed from both sides of the comparison) was caught twice in the same month — once in a workhorse's analysis, and once, days later, in the verifier's own.
- **Coverage × accuracy, not accuracy alone.** Restated as the project's clearest single mistake avoided (or rather, only avoided in retrospect): a model correct 72.8% of the time about 39% of the repertoire is worse, in expectation, than one correct 59.2% of the time about 79% of it — "nobody computed this for a month and a half, and it inverted the conclusion when they did."
- **Selection-on-the-same-data-you-then-score-on inflates results by a measured, not assumed, amount** — quantified at roughly 4.4 percentage points in one specific admission-threshold experiment, and treated as a general hazard elsewhere.
- **When a handoff document asserts something is broken, check it before repeating it.** The clearest cautionary tale: a claim that `pytest tests/` was fundamentally unrunnable because a Python package didn't exist was written once, believed, and silently copied forward into every subsequent handoff for six weeks — the actual problem, discovered and fixed on August 13, was a 30-second fix (the package existed; it just wasn't on `sys.path`). Roughly 95 tests went unrun for over a month on the strength of an unverified sentence.

## 19. Current state of the code, as of the most recent commit (Aug 14, 2026)

Summarizing `handoff.md`'s own "one-line version," and the state of the working tree at time of writing:

The **composition-identification path is considered fully closed** — five independent approaches (lyrics-ASR precision, ASR fine-tuning, melodic CSI, duration, Qmax) were each tried, measured, and killed on hard evidence, and the project's explicit instruction to any future agent is not to reopen any of them "without genuinely new information." The **raga path survived every audit thrown at it** and was substantially widened on August 13 to a new 54-class fused model (`models/raga_v4_*`), measured at 59.2% top-1 / 72.8% top-3 / 79.2% repertoire coverage / 46.86 expected correct answers per 100 concert items — a real, audited improvement over both the 32-class tier the product would ship today if wired in (39.12 expected hits/100) and the original 18-class tier most of the project's history was built on (28.2–28.75). **This new model has deliberately not been wired into `identify_clip.py` or deployed anywhere** — Deepti's explicit instruction on August 13 was to make the underlying tooling robust first (the raw audio-feature pipeline is currently duplicated, with subtle parameter drift, across eleven separate files; there is no single "score this audio" function anywhere in the codebase; two different evaluation harnesses disagree by a small but now-quantified amount because they count rows per track differently; and the test suite, only discovered to be runnable at all on August 13, still has three known failing tests).

The single most valuable pending measurement in the whole project — repeatedly described that way in the most recent handoff — is **scoring the raga model against Deepti's own 11 long-form concert recordings**, which is blocked purely on her labeling the roughly 100–140 individual musical items they contain; no algorithm-driven segmentation is being used, on the deliberate grounds that an automatic segmenter would itself be an unvalidated confound sitting between the audio and the raga number.

The working branch, `probe/duration-and-negative-arm`, sits roughly 55 commits ahead of `main` and has not been pushed to the remote repository — this is repeatedly, explicitly *not* treated as a problem or a blocker anywhere in the project's own documentation; pushing and deploying are activities the project's own rules reserve entirely for Deepti, on her own schedule.
